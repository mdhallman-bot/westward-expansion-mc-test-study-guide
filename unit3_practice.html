Vocabulary Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check the definition.

Manifest destiny
The belief that America was destined (by God, in O'Sullivan's words) to expand across the whole continent.
Push factor
A hardship or danger that drives people away from a place.
Pull factor
Something that draws people toward a new place.
Homestead
160 acres of public land the Homestead Act (1862) offered free to anyone who lived on and farmed it for 5 years.
Subsidy
Government financial support given to a private company.
Allotment
Dividing reservation land into individual plots for Native families, instead of shared tribal ownership.
Surplus land
Land "left over" after allotment, which the government then sold — often to non-Native buyers.
Tribal sovereignty
A tribal nation's own right to govern itself and its land.
Popular sovereignty
Voters deciding an issue themselves — a different concept from tribal sovereignty, easy to confuse.
Discrimination
Unequal treatment based on group membership.
Labor recruitment
Actively seeking out and persuading people to relocate for work.
Nativism
The belief that immigrants threaten an established population.
Naturalization
The legal process of becoming a citizen.
Exoduster
A Black Southerner who migrated to Kansas in 1879–80, naming the movement after the Bible's Exodus — an escape from oppression toward a promised land.
Corroborate
To check two sources against each other to see where they agree or disagree.
Perspective
The vantage point that shapes how someone experiences and tells a story — always ask whose is missing.
Assimilation
Forcing a group to abandon its own culture and adopt another — different from segregation (separating groups) or self-determination (self-governing).
Cause and effect
What happened first, and what it led to — order matters.
Promise vs. reality
What someone was told would happen, compared to what actually happened.

Topic Quick Review

Click a topic to expand it.

American Progress (Gast, 1872) shows Columbia leading settlers west while Native Americans and buffalo flee — one perspective, not a neutral record.
Free land was a real pull factor, but “free” still required money for tools, seed, and animals — many claims were abandoned.
Broke reservations into individual plots; leftover “surplus” land was sold off. Native landholding fell from ~138 million to ~48 million acres by 1934.
Government subsidies funded railroads → cheap hide shipping → mass hunting → herds collapsed (tens of millions to ~1,100 by 1889) → Plains nations pushed toward reservations.
Chinese crews were most of the workforce, paid less, largely absent from the 1869 completion photo. The 1867 strike for equal pay ended when the company cut off food. The 1882 Exclusion Act banned new laborers and blocked citizenship — valued when needed, shut out once the work was done.
Singleton's 1878 broadside recruited Black Southerners to Kansas, warning them to “beware of speculators.” Push factor: violence and lost rights after Reconstruction ended (1877). Nicodemus, KS survived but stalled once railroads bypassed it.
The 1868 Fort Laramie Treaty promised the Black Hills for Lakota use — broken when gold drew miners in 1874. Newspapers calling Little Bighorn (1876) a “massacre” reveals whose side they favored. Chief Joseph's 1877 surrender speech was translated by an Army officer, not written by Joseph himself.
Sand Creek (1864): a peace-signaling camp attacked anyway. Dakota War (1862): late treaty payments → hunger → violence → 38 Dakota men executed. Carlisle (opened 1879): Pratt's goal was to “kill the Indian… save the man” — the method shifted from military force to erasing culture, not a change in underlying goal.
